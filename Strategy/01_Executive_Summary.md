# Executive Summary

## 1. Executive Diagnosis

The customer base differs much more in Repeat-Purchase History than in Current Observed Spend. Average Current Observed Spend is tightly grouped across the six behavioral segments ($59.24–$60.93), while average Repeat-Purchase History ranges from 6.5 for Early-Stage Customers to 36.6 for Core Loyalists. The immediate strategic opportunity is therefore to understand and influence repeat behavior, not to assume that the current basket separates strong and weak customer relationships.

Observed Promo Exposure covers 1,677 of 3,900 customers (43.0%), but those customers are not one uniform group. A selective, controlled approach is warranted; blanket promotional removal is not supported by this snapshot.

## 2. Strongest findings

1. **Behavioral depth is the primary separator.** Promo-Engaged Repeaters have an average Repeat-Purchase History of 31.4, compared with 16.2 for Promo-Exposed Transactors, even though their average Current Observed Spend is nearly identical ($59.44 versus $59.24).
2. **Current promo users are heterogeneous.** The two fully promo-exposed segments differ materially in Repeat-Purchase History, stated purchase frequency, satisfaction, and subscription mix. Observed Promo Exposure alone is not sufficient for treatment decisions.
3. **Behavioral Loyalty Proxy and Commercial Value Proxy answer different questions.** The former ranks repeat behavior, stated frequency, satisfaction, and low Observed Promo Exposure; the latter incorporates Current Observed Spend. Neither is a longitudinal loyalty measure.
4. **Geography identifies test cells, not proven growth markets.** State-level differences exist, but each state is a small cross-sectional sample and the data contains no acquisition costs, market share, time trend, or causal evidence.
5. **Category differences are modest.** Average Repeat-Purchase History spans only 25.0–25.7 across categories, and average Current Observed Spend spans $57.17–$60.26. Category should provide context, not drive broad strategic claims.

## 3. What this means for the business

The business should replace broad discount rules with testable customer treatments. Higher-history current promo users are the best initial learning population because they combine meaningful Repeat-Purchase History with current Observed Promo Exposure. Lower-history current promo users should be protected from abrupt changes until response can be observed. Geography can be used to create matched test and control cells, while category and demographics should mainly support balance checks and interpretation.

## 4. Recommended actions

- Run a small randomized promotional-sunset pilot among Promo-Engaged Repeaters, using a holdout that retains the current treatment.
- Expand only when purchase behavior is non-inferior to control and satisfaction and service guardrails remain stable.
- Instrument transaction timestamps, promotion type and discount depth, gross margin, acquisition source, customer acquisition date, and order-level category.
- Build repeat-purchase and cohort baselines before making broader customer-value or geographic investment decisions.
- Use the Behavioral Loyalty Proxy for behavioral prioritization and the Commercial Value Proxy for current commercial context; do not treat either as a future-outcome guarantee.

## 5. Key risks and limitations

This is a customer-level cross-sectional snapshot with no transaction timestamps. It cannot establish actual retention, customer departure, historical promotional behavior, or causal effects. Stated purchase frequency is not transaction-derived. The observed category is one purchase per customer, and state-level comparisons do not measure market potential. Contribution margin cannot be evaluated without cost and discount data. All rollout decisions therefore require controlled experimentation and longitudinal tracking.
