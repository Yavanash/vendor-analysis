# 30/60/90-Day Implementation Plan

## Days 0–30: Build measurement and experiment readiness

| Objective | Actions | KPI | Owner/function | Key risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Create reliable longitudinal measurement and a pre-registered test design | Capture order and event timestamps, discount amount/depth, promotion type, gross margin inputs, acquisition source, order-level category, and customer acquisition date; define customer identity rules; document treatment eligibility, randomization, control, observation window, non-inferiority margin, guardrails, and reversal rules | Required-field completeness; customer/order match rate; assignment logging coverage; approved experiment specification | Data/Analytics with Engineering; Growth/CRM; Finance; Customer Experience | Missing or inconsistent identifiers and cost fields can make results uninterpretable |
| Establish auditable baselines | Preserve the current segment assignment as the starting snapshot; define repeat-purchase incidence, order frequency, Current Observed Spend, satisfaction, returns, cancellations, and support-contact measurements without changing existing formulas | Baseline report completed; metric definitions approved; reconciliation to source systems | Analytics and Finance | Retrospective data may be insufficient because the current dataset has no timestamps |

## Days 31–60: Run controlled learning programs

| Objective | Actions | KPI | Owner/function | Key risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Launch the promotional-sunset pilot | Randomize eligible Promo-Engaged Repeaters into treatment and control; reduce or suppress the planned discount only in treatment; monitor assignment integrity and exposure | Repeat-purchase incidence versus control under the predefined non-inferiority rule | Growth/CRM and Analytics, with Finance and Customer Experience | Small samples, contamination, seasonality, or incomplete exposure logging may distort the result |
| Run a geographic test | Select matched state or market cells using adequate sample size and comparable baseline behavior; test a defined message, offer, or channel treatment; retain control | Predefined behavioral KPI versus matched control; treatment delivery rate | Growth/Marketing and Analytics | State-level snapshot differences may not generalize or may reflect unmeasured factors |
| Establish the first longitudinal repeat-purchase baseline and monitor guardrails | Measure customer orders over the fixed window; track satisfaction, returns, cancellations, support contacts, delivery issues, and gross margin where available | Baseline coverage and timeliness; guardrail status; data-quality exceptions | Analytics, Customer Experience, Operations, Finance | The observation window may be too short for customers with slower purchase cadence |

## Days 61–90: Scale evidence and improve decision models

| Objective | Actions | KPI | Owner/function | Key risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scale only successful treatments | Expand only cells that remain non-inferior to control and within guardrails; maintain a persistent holdout; reverse failing cells | Primary KPI and guardrails versus holdout; rollout coverage among validated cells | Growth/CRM, Analytics, Finance, Customer Experience | Early wins may not persist at broader scale or across subgroups |
| Build cohort and promotion history | Create acquisition and first-order cohorts; track repeat purchases by elapsed time; build customer-level promotion exposure, type, discount depth, and redemption history | Cohort coverage; timestamp completeness; promotion-to-order linkage rate | Data/Analytics and Engineering | Identity resolution or late-arriving events can bias cohort comparisons |
| Create a margin-aware customer value model | Combine order revenue with product, fulfillment, return, and discount costs; validate model outputs and document assumptions | Share of orders with complete contribution inputs; finance reconciliation; validation status | Finance and Analytics | Cost allocation may be incomplete or inconsistent; model outputs must not be presented as guaranteed future value |
| Refine the ICP using future observed behavior | Reassess which starting signals predict subsequent repeat behavior and margin outcomes; recalibrate targeting only after validation | Out-of-sample predictive performance, calibration, and subgroup stability | Analytics with Growth/Marketing | Overfitting the initial 90-day window or turning proxy relationships into causal claims |

## Operating principle

No treatment should scale because of a favorable snapshot association alone. Scale requires a predefined comparison with control, adequate measurement, stable guardrails, and finance-ready cost data. Cohort retention and customer promo history become measurable only after longitudinal records have accumulated; they are not available from the current cross-sectional dataset.
